## One-line PR slice

Measure `MAX_EVENT_DATA_LEN`, `MAX_TAGS_PER_EVENT` and `MAX_EVENTS_PER_BATCH` against a real Durable Object under the `workerd`/`wasm32` conformance run and write them into `impl Fixture for CloudflareFixture` as facts, settle `REOPEN` and `MID_BATCH_FAULT` against what that runner actually permits, and thereby turn `append_reports_exceeded_store_limits` from a rule that skips with `NO_STORE_LIMITS` into a rule that reaches the store and passes at both boundaries.

## Executive summary

HS-S0053 lands the fixture with all five of these answers written out **deliberately but provisionally** — the shape, with this story named as the owner of the values (`../durable-object-host-and-fixture/spec.md:348`). HS-S0054 lands the target that executes every event-store rule against that fixture inside one `cargo xtask ci`. This PR is the delta that makes the last rule in that run non-vacuous: three numbers located by measurement against the real runner, and two capability verdicts confirmed or overturned against it.

The delta is small in lines and large in meaning. `Fixture`'s three ceilings are the only place in the conformance contract where a fixture makes a **quantitative** promise, and the promise is symmetric — declare `N` and `append_reports_exceeded_store_limits` requires exactly `N` accepted and `N + 1` refused as `AppendError::ExceedsStoreLimit` naming the matching `StoreLimit`, with nothing of the refused value left in the log (`crates/happenstance-testkit/src/suite.rs:4330-4375`, `:4378-4434`, `:4437-4480`). A guessed number therefore fails in one direction or the other, which is the rule doing its job. What no existing check catches is the other move: leave all three at their `None` defaults, and the rule reports `Skipped { capability: NO_STORE_LIMITS }`, prints an honest reason, and certifies nothing — while the one runtime in this workspace with a documented hard SQL storage cap contributes nothing at all to the clause it was brought in to discharge (`crates/happenstance-cloudflare/src/sql_storage.rs:119-121`). `spec/SPECIFICATION.md:7694-7698` names that exact shape as what CF-40 exists to prevent: *"a suite in which **no** fixture states a ceiling, where `append_reports_exceeded_store_limits` skips everywhere and VT-25 is a variant nothing exercises."*

This PR also produces the **evidence package**, not the record: three numbers, how each was obtained, and the CF-40 ownership finding go to `adr-0023-and-atom-resolutions` (HS-S0059), which is the only story in this project permitted to write to `.kb/`.

## Context pack

Everything below is a decision this PR must honour. Deeper material sits behind the anchors the second half of this spec carries.

**A stated ceiling is a promise checked at both ends, so it cannot be read off a documentation page.** CF-40 `[PROVISIONAL]` (`spec/SPECIFICATION.md:7661-7684`) requires the three ceilings as `Option<usize>` associated constants and makes a stated value binding in both directions. A published platform figure is a legitimate *starting point* for the probe and is never the declaration: the declaration is a promise about **this adapter's** behaviour at the boundary, and an adapter can be narrower than its platform because of its own schema — the tags table, the parameter count a batch statement renders, the envelope columns `durable-object-write-path` added. Measure the adapter, not the platform.

**`None` is a statement, not an absence, and for this runtime it is a false one.** The constants default to `None` on the trait (`crates/happenstance-testkit/src/contract.rs:253`, `:262`, `:279`), and the trait is explicit that `None` means *this store has no ceiling* — a fact about the store, not a trade, which is why it is not spelled `Capability` and why the skip carries the testkit's own `NO_CEILING_REASON` rather than a reason the fixture wrote (`contract.rs:214-247`, `:442`, `:454-456`). Inheriting that default here would put a sentence in the CI log that is untrue of a store whose backing API has `SqlError::StorageLimitExceeded` in its error enum.

**The default is the mutant, and it cannot be caught by a store.** The four declared-but-wrong shapes are already compiled and registered — `PayloadCeilingStore` and `BatchParameterCeilingStore` refuse through `AppendError::Store`, `TruncatingPayloadStore` and `ChunkLosingBatchStore` do not refuse at all (`crates/happenstance-testkit/tests/mutation_coverage.rs:1919-2040`; `spec/SPECIFICATION.md:7690-7698`). This story adds **no** mutant and **no** rule: the `None` failure is in the *fixture's declaration*, not in a store's behaviour, so no store can fail it and `CLAUDE.md`'s corollary forbids writing a rule that nothing can fail. Its detector is a named review obligation — the CF-29-shaped changelog entry and the ADR-0023 record of the three numbers and how each was obtained — so a reader can tell a measurement from an omission.

**A ceiling below a floor is a conformance failure, never a declaration.** `MIN_SUPPORTED_EVENT_DATA_LEN = 65_536`, `MIN_SUPPORTED_TAGS_PER_EVENT = 64`, `MIN_SUPPORTED_EVENTS_PER_BATCH = 128` (`crates/happenstance-core/src/limits.rs:22`, `:27`, `:43`) are floors every store must clear, checked by `store_accepts_the_guaranteed_minimum_payload` (`suite.rs:3775`), `…_tag_count` (`:3829`) and `…_batch_size` (`:3954`). The trait says in terms that a store *may* state a ceiling below the floor and will then fail those rules, correctly (`contract.rs:249-252`). So a measurement that comes in under a floor is not a smaller number to declare: it is a schema or batching change in the adapter, or a blocking finding escalated to `RUNBOOK.md`'s phase 9 and ADR-0023.

**A cumulative cap is not a per-append ceiling, and mistaking the two fires CF-40's own falsifier.** `SqlError::StorageLimitExceeded` (`crates/happenstance-cloudflare/src/sql_storage.rs:119-121`) is the *object's* SQL storage limit — a function of everything already stored — while `MAX_EVENT_DATA_LEN` is a per-value boundary the rule probes with an at-the-ceiling append followed by a one-larger append against the same store. Declaring a cumulative cap as a per-value constant would make the at-the-ceiling anchor pass or fail depending on what the preceding rules wrote. CF-40's `[PROVISIONAL]` marker names precisely this as its falsifier — *"a real adapter whose ceiling is not a constant"* — and names the Cloudflare adapter at phase 9 as one of its three instruments (`spec/SPECIFICATION.md:7676-7684`). If the only real cap here is cumulative, the honest output is `None` for that limit **plus** a written falsification finding for ADR-0023, not a number that happens to pass once.

**`MID_BATCH_FAULT`: a real armed seam or an honest decline — never a claimed capability with an empty arm.** CF-39 `[PROVISIONAL]` (`spec/SPECIFICATION.md:7631-7660`) requires a fixture declaring it supported to cause the *k*-th write to fail **inside the store's own write path by a mechanism the store cannot absorb**, and to state the mechanism; a fixture whose store absorbs every fault it can arm MUST decline with that as its reason. The named wrong implementation is `NoopFaultFixture` — supported, with `arm_mid_batch_fault` overridden by an empty body, which never reaches the trait's panic and reports a green atomicity result for a store that has never been faulted. A Durable Object can plausibly arm one (a `CHECK` constraint or trigger armed for exactly one write), which would make this the **first** adapter in the workspace to exercise CF-39 for real — new *conformance suite* coverage, not just new adapter coverage, and the reason the changelog obligation lands on this story (`../_decomposition.md:630-637`).

**`REOPEN` is a re-read, not a re-decision.** HS-S0053 declares it with the trait's own Durable-Object reasoning — storage outlives the isolate, so handle state can be discarded and the store read again, while restarting the isolate from inside a test is not possible (`contract.rs:163-173`). This story confirms the declaration survived contact with the real runner and overturns it only on evidence; overturning it is a one-line constant change by construction, which is why HS-S0053 wrote the constants out rather than inheriting them (`../durable-object-host-and-fixture/spec.md:460`).

**CF-40's ownership is not this story's to mint, and the finding it hands over is sharper than the question.** `.kb/open-questions/cf-40-fixture-limits-ownership.md` records that ADR-0015 both claims and disclaims the clause, that ADR-0012 is the other claimant by adjacency, and that the forcing phase is phase 8 — `sqlite-durable-store` (HS-P0012), which merges one position **ahead** of this project. Neither project may mint an answer; whichever reaches it first owns it and the other cites it (`../_decomposition.md:447-454`). What this story can settle is narrower and is itself the finding: declaring three numbers here was **not** blocked by not knowing which document owns CF-40, because the clause's text is identical under either reading — which narrows the atom's question from *which ADR owns CF-40* to its own sub-question 2, whether the fixture contract has one owning document at all.

**No accepted atom is edited and no KB file is written here.** Accepted decision atoms are immutable and `redkiln validate --kb` checks them against `HEAD` (`CLAUDE.md`; `.kb/decisions/README.md`). Every KB write in this project belongs to `adr-0023-and-atom-resolutions` (`../_storymap.md:94-102`).

**The persona slice.** The story map's three observers are the adapter author, the gate reader and the library consumer (`../_storymap.md:24-30`). This story serves the **gate reader** at activity D — *know what this runtime cannot do, and why*. Before it, that reader sees one more honest-looking skip line among the real ones. After it, they see the capacity rule execute against a store with a named boundary, and the skip lines that remain are the ones this runtime genuinely earns.

## Data and migrations

**No schema change, no migration, and that is a constraint rather than an absence.**

The measurement runs against the schema `durable-object-write-path` (HS-S0050) established through `CloudflareEventStore::migrate()`, the schema seam the fixture calls once per instance (`crates/happenstance-cloudflare/src/event_store.rs`; Architecture §4a, `../_decomposition.md:282-290`). Three consequences follow and each is load-bearing:

1. **The numbers describe that schema, so the schema may not drift under them.** A widened value column, a change to how tags are stored, or a different batch rendering moves at least one ceiling. If HS-S0050's schema changes after these constants land, the constants are re-measured, not re-guessed — and `append_reports_exceeded_store_limits` will say so in one direction or the other before anyone gets the chance to guess.
2. **A sub-floor measurement is the one circumstance in which this story would want a schema change, and it does not take one unilaterally.** Raising a ceiling by widening a column or chunking an insert is HS-S0050's file and HS-S0050's decision; the response here is to stop and escalate (a blocking finding to phase 9 and ADR-0023), because a fixture is never excused from a floor by declaring a smaller number (`crates/happenstance-testkit/src/contract.rs:249-252`).
3. **Storage state is isolated per measurement, per the fixture's own invariant.** One fixture instance is one Durable Object's storage and two instances share nothing — itself a conformance rule (`crates/happenstance-testkit/src/contract.rs:17-23`; Architecture §4d). The probe therefore opens a fresh instance for each boundary search rather than reusing one across searches, both because a cumulative storage cap would otherwise contaminate a per-value measurement and because a probe that leaves rows behind changes what a later rule reads.

No data is persisted by this story outside a Durable Object created and discarded by the harness, and no fixture data, seed or backfill is introduced.

## Implementation notes (non-prescriptive)

- **Order the work so the measurement is the last thing that is guessed at.** Land the slice-mates first (they are dependencies for a reason), get the suite executing with the three constants still at their defaults, and read the `NO_STORE_LIMITS` skip line with your own eyes. That line is the story's *before* picture, and it is worth pasting into the report before it disappears.
- **Search, do not binary-search blindly.** A published platform figure is a legitimate seed for the search; the search is what turns it into a declaration. Bracket from the seed, then narrow. Each candidate is checked on a **fresh fixture instance** (Data and migrations §3) so a cumulative cap cannot contaminate a per-value answer.
- **Measure the adapter, not the platform.** The subtraction that matters is this adapter's own overhead: envelope columns, the tags table, and the parameter count the batch statement renders. If a declared number is exactly a round platform figure, that is a signal the subtraction was not done.
- **Prefer a small honest margin to a fragile exact maximum, and say so.** Nothing in CF-40 requires the declared ceiling to be the largest value the store could ever accept — only that the declared value is accepted and one more is refused. If the true boundary is unstable at the last few bytes, declaring a slightly lower number and *documenting that it is a stated ceiling rather than the physical maximum* is honest; declaring an unstable exact maximum is how a green run becomes a flaky one.
- **For `MID_BATCH_FAULT`, write the negative control before the arm.** Arm the seam, watch `append_is_atomic_under_a_mid_batch_fault` fail for the right reason, then make it pass. A fault seam that has never been observed to make a rule go red is indistinguishable from `NoopFaultFixture` from the outside.
- **Read `MemoryFixture`'s declined-capability wording before writing a decline** (`crates/happenstance-testkit/src/fixtures.rs:242-292`, and `:280-286` for the shape of an honest reason). The bar is *why this runtime cannot*, not *that it cannot*.
- **Write the evidence package as you go, not at the end.** Its consumer is a different story (HS-S0058) run by a different context; a derivation reconstructed from memory a week later is the thing AC-010 says a reader must be able to distinguish from an omission.
- **Keep the crate documentation's numbers and the impl's constants in one place if you can** — a doc comment on the constant that `cargo doc` renders is one fact; a paragraph elsewhere repeating the number is two facts that can disagree.

## Risks and coupling (PR-scoped)

- **Blast radius.** Files touched: `crates/happenstance-cloudflare/src/lib.rs` (the fixture impl's constants, at most one override, crate docs), `crates/happenstance-cloudflare/tests/fixture_contract.rs`, `experiments/durable-object-limits/**`, `CHANGELOG.md`, and this story's own folder. **Not** touched: `crates/happenstance-testkit/**`, `spec/SPECIFICATION.md`, `.kb/**`, `xtask/**`, any other crate.
- **Coupling to `durable-object-write-path` (HS-S0050) is the tightest edge and runs both ways.** The numbers describe HS-S0050's schema, so a later schema change invalidates them — and EC-003 says a mis-classified refusal is escalated to HS-S0050 rather than absorbed by moving a number. The mitigating fact is that `append_reports_exceeded_store_limits` fails in one direction or the other if the schema drifts under the constants, so drift is loud rather than silent.
- **Coupling to the slice-mates is total by design.** The measurement and its verification are the same run: without HS-S0054's registered target there is nowhere to measure, and without HS-S0053's fixture there is nothing to declare on. This story is last in the slice for that reason and cannot be pulled forward.
- **CF-40 double-mint with `sqlite-durable-store` (HS-P0012), which merges one position ahead.** Neither project may mint the ownership answer independently. Mitigation is EC-008 plus the explicit coordination check in AC-011's evidence package; the residual risk is a race in which both write to `.kb/_intake/` — which is why neither this story nor HS-P0012's equivalent may write `.kb/` at all, and both hand to a single ingest.
- **The quiet failure mode is doing nothing.** Every check in the tree is satisfied by leaving all three constants at `None`. AC-004's adapter-local test is the one mechanical guard; the changelog entry and the ADR-0023 record are the human ones. If the adapter-local test is ever deleted as redundant, this risk is fully restored.
- **A supported `MID_BATCH_FAULT` is new coverage and therefore new risk.** It is the first time these two rules run for real anywhere in the workspace; if they turn out to be wrong, `CLAUDE.md`'s rule applies — fix the rule and explain why in the same change — but that is a testkit change and would be a finding to escalate, not silent work absorbed into this PR.
- **Timing.** A payload append at a large declared ceiling runs on every gate invocation on every runner in the matrix. NF-002 keeps it bounded; if the ceiling turns out to be large enough that the rule's own work is measurable, say so in the report rather than shrinking the declaration to make CI faster.

## Clarifications resolved during spec

1. **The story-id references in the front half are corrected here.** The scope lock and PR boundary name `adr-0023-and-atom-resolutions` as **HS-S0059**; the item files say otherwise — `adr-0023-and-atom-resolutions` is **HS-S0058** and `publish-ready-crate` is **HS-S0059** (`../adr-0023-and-atom-resolutions/story.md:2`, `../publish-ready-crate/story.md:2`), which is also what this story's own `blocks: [HS-S0058, HS-S0059]` means. 2. **`MID_BATCH_FAULT` is settled as a *conditional* verdict, not a preference.** The discover stage deferred it to spec jointly with HS-S0053. It is resolved as AC-008's disjunction — a real unabsorbable seam with its mechanism stated, or a decline naming why the store absorbs every fault this host can arm — with the negative control (arm removed, atomicity rule observed red) as the thing that separates the two from the outside. The spec deliberately does not pre-commit to which arm, because that answer belongs to the runner and not to a planning document.
3. **A sub-floor measurement is resolved as an escalation, not a declaration** (EC-001). Discover deferred the detail to spec; the shape is now: schema or batching change owned by HS-S0050, or a blocking finding to phase 9 and ADR-0023.
4. **The `None`-default defect gets an *adapter-local* test, which is not a conformance rule and does not contradict the Behavior table.** `CLAUDE.md` forbids adding a **conformance rule** no adapter can fail; `crates/happenstance-cloudflare/tests/fixture_contract.rs::the_three_store_limits_are_measured_not_defaulted` is a test of *this fixture's declaration*, lives in this crate, ships in no suite and gates no other adapter.
