## One-line PR slice

Write and accept **ADR-0017** (what a projection batch owns and what vocabulary writes into it, PS-4 – PS-15), **ADR-0018** (how a projection returns to "never run", its transactional scope, and what may refuse it, PS-16 – PS-20) and **ADR-0019** (what happens when `apply` fails, PS-26 – PS-30) as `.kb/decisions/` atoms — each quoting a compiler transcript from `references/adapter-shapes.md` by `file:line`, each naming the alternatives that lost, ADR-0017 naming `LiveHandleProjectionStore`'s disposition — with the one open-question atom they answer marked **resolved rather than deleted**, the six they deliberately do not answer named with their owners, and `redkiln validate --kb` green **before** any line of `crates/happenstance-core/src/projection.rs` changes.

## Executive summary

This PR lands **a decision record and nothing else**. No crate is touched, no clause is edited, no maturity marker moves, and `cargo xtask spec-trace` sees the same tree it saw before. What changes is the knowledge base: three long-form ADRs under `references/adr/`, staged into `.kb/_intake/` and folded into three accepted `decision` atoms by one `/redkiln:kb-ingest` wave, plus one open-question resolution and the map rows that make all four findable.

The delta over `project.md` and the briefs — the part an implementer cannot read off the charter:

- **The charter says the ADRs come first; this spec says how they are authored.** `.kb/decisions/` atoms are *never hand-written* in this repository (`CLAUDE.md`, *Where the work lives*): they are produced by `/redkiln:kb-ingest` from `.kb/_intake/`, because hand-writing them "produces the directory layout of the process without the process", which is why the first attempt was reverted at `0269720`. So the story's own writes are the long-form records and the intake staging; the atoms are an *output* of a command the human invokes.
- **The ADRs do not decide the clause text.** `spec/SPECIFICATION.md` already states the target trait in full (`:4632-4731`) and eleven `[FROZEN]` PS clauses presuppose it. An ADR in this corpus is *history* — why the fork went the way it did, and what lost — and "an ADR is never updated to match the code" (`.kb/decisions/README.md`). The work here is reconstructing the reasoning and the rejected alternatives from the evidence base, not re-litigating the shape.
- **Exactly one open question is answered.** The temptation is to sweep the projection-shaped open questions closed while three ADRs are open on the desk. Six of them are owned elsewhere and are named below so the omission reads as a decision rather than an oversight.
- **The ordering *is* the acceptance criterion.** AC-008's check is a commit sequence, not a schema check (`_decomposition.md` Testing brief, AC-008 row). An atom accepted after the port changed proves nothing about which one was allowed to constrain the other.

## Context pack

Everything below is a decision this story must honor. Deeper material sits behind the anchors table.

### 1. What an ADR is here, and what it is not

A decision atom commits the project — *must*, *shall*, *must not* — states the alternatives that lost, and is **immutable once accepted**: `redkiln validate --kb` checks each `status: accepted` decision body against `HEAD`, so a correction is a new atom carrying `supersedes`, never a reword (`.kb/decisions/README.md`). The evidence a decision rests on is *not* in the decision — it is a `reference` atom or a cited file, "separating them is what lets a decision be superseded without invalidating the evidence underneath it". And the decision is not current truth: `spec/SPECIFICATION.md` says what is true now, and where the two disagree the specification wins.

Two consequences that bite immediately. **Quote, do not paraphrase, the transcripts** — cite `references/adapter-shapes.md` by `file:line` the way `references/adr/0009-error-send-sync.md` does, because the record exists so the next reader does not re-derive it. And **one decision per title**: each of the three runbook questions is a conjunction, and `kb-playbook-one-decision-per-adr-title-001` records that three times in two days an "and" in an ADR title carried a strong decision and a weak one, and the weak one was reversed within days. The discriminator is not the conjunction — it is what each half rests on. A half settled by *being made* stands; a half that is a claim about code that does not exist yet is a guess. Where a half is a guess, mark it provisional with the observation that would refute it and the phase that would produce it, or split the document.

### 2. ADR-0017 — the batch, and the seam that is not a write vocabulary

The open question this closes asks whether the apply seam is a bound on `Batch`, a second associated type, or a method on `ProjectionStore` (`kb-open-question-projection-batch-no-apply-001`, sub-question 1). **The answer is none of the three, and the shape is a split by consumer.** Do not flatten this to "the port grows a write method":

- **PS-9** (`spec/SPECIFICATION.md:4947-4962`) — `Batch` MUST NOT carry a universal write vocabulary; a projection writes through the concrete adapter's inherent API. What loses is `fn put(&mut self, key: &str, value: &[u8])` on a `ProjectionBatch` supertrait: it obliges a graph store and a relational store each to carry a key-value table nobody asked for, and it reintroduces at the read-model layer the opaque blob ADR-0003 confined to event payloads. An application's runner never needed the vocabulary — ADR-0007's pump compiles against the port unchanged, which is why ADR-0007's Context overstates itself (`spec/SPECIFICATION.md:4925-4944`).
- **PS-11** (`:4977-5031`) — the *conformance suite* needs one, because it is generic over an adapter it has never seen. So the seam is `ProjectionProbe: ProjectionStore` in the **contract crate** behind `feature = "conformance"`, bare flavour only. It is not a testkit trait for a coherence reason that is expensive to rediscover: an adapter's `tests/` directory is a different crate, where neither a testkit trait nor the adapter's type is local, so the impl is rejected by the orphan rule and the adapter is forced into a non-dev dependency on `happenstance-testkit`. In the contract crate it costs one feature flag on a dependency the adapter already has and no new edge in the graph.

**Batch ownership: `type Batch;`, no lifetime — and the clause must rest on the right argument.** The `Send` argument (rusqlite's `Connection` is `Send` but not `Sync`, `Transaction<'_>` is `!Send`, six diagnostics carrying **no error code at all**, `references/adapter-shapes.md:61-102`) is *one-driver-wide*, and `crates/happenstance-ladybug/src/live_handle.rs:14-31` says so in the tree: LadybugDB's `Connection` is `Send` *and* `Sync`, so a genuinely borrowed `GraphWriteHandle<'a>` binds to `type Batch<'a>` on the `Send` flavour with real bodies. PS-5's *other* argument is untouched by that instrument and is stronger: today's port is implementable **only by stores that outlive every batch lifetime**, and a non-`'static` store crashes rustc in the error-reporting path with `DefId::expect_local` (`references/adapter-shapes.md:307-365`, `live_handle.rs:36-66`) — plus `error[E0195]`, which binding an owned type does not relieve while the trait still declares `Self::Batch<'_>` (`references/adapter-shapes.md:186-194`). An ADR-0017 that rests the clause on `Send` has been refuted by an instrument already in the tree.

**Two things the ADR must carry forward rather than claim.** Dropping the lifetime does **not** close the foreign-batch hazard: tying the batch to the receiver's lifetime was compiled and refuted — a lifetime names a region, not an instance — and only a generative brand rejects `b.commit(a.begin())`, at the cost of fighting `async` and forbidding the batch escaping a closure. PS-15 stays `[PROVISIONAL]` and is discharged at run time by `CommitError::ForeignBatch` (`spec/SPECIFICATION.md:5099-5155`). And **`LiveHandleProjectionStore`'s disposition is decided here and named** — deleted, moved to `experiments/`, or kept with its transcripts and a note that the port no longer admits it. Deleting the only compiled evidence against the decision you are making is how a port gets frozen against its own hypothesis (`_decomposition.md` Architecture brief AC-A05, Note 7).

### 3. ADR-0018 — reset, its scope, and what may refuse it

Four decisions, of three different strengths, and the ADR must not level them:

- **`reset(batch, id)` is one unit of work** — apply the batch and return `id`'s checkpoint to `NeverRun` together (PS-16, `[PROVISIONAL]`, `:5172-5186`). What it rejects is the two-statement runbook procedure on two connections; the ADR should carry the failure it models rather than assert atomicity abstractly. `reset` takes the *caller's own deletes* because the port has no idea what the read model is (`:5165-5170`), and `probe_delete_all` exists so the suite can exercise it without knowing either.
- **Scoped to one `(store, ProjectionId)`** — PS-17, `[FROZEN]`, and explicitly *the same decision* ADR-0007 already made for checkpoints, applied to the operation that removes one (`:5188-5199`). This half is settled by prior decision, not by this ADR; say so rather than re-deriving it.
- **Refusal is a port mechanism with the policy in the domain** — PS-18, `[PROVISIONAL]`, `:5200-5217`. The alternative that lost is named in the clause: refusal as purely a typed-layer concern. That is where the *policy* belongs, but "a policy with no port-level mechanism is bypassed by anyone holding the store, which is every operator with a runbook."
- **`Checkpoint` is a three-variant enum**, so `NeverRun` is distinguishable from `commit(empty, id, FIRST, Live)` (PS-19/PS-20). The tuple `(Option<SequencePosition>, bool)` loses because it can spell `(None, true)` — "authoritative, never run — which means nothing" (`:4643-4658`).

**The trap.** PS-19 sits inside this ADR's clause range and carries a known pairing defect: its MUST is scoped *after a successful reset*, while the rule `fresh_projection_has_no_checkpoint` asks about an id never seen, and the store that satisfies one and fails the other is the natural implementation (`:5218-5249`; `kb-open-question-ps-19-scope-narrower-001`). **ADR-0018 does not repair it.** The repair is a frozen-clause repair, it is a new decision atom under `kb-playbook-repair-frozen-clause-001`'s discipline, and it lands at `unstable-projection-gate-and-clause-disposition` on the sweep's evidence. What ADR-0018 owes is a sentence stating that the defect is known, scoped out, and whose it is. The mechanical test for the line: *a correction to a `[FROZEN]` clause is a repair if the set of implementations the clause admits is unchanged; otherwise it is a gap, and a gap is a decision's.*

### 4. ADR-0019 — apply failure, and the scope line that is easy to cross

PS-26 – PS-30 are almost entirely about a **runner that does not exist yet and is not this project's** (`project.md` Out of scope: the `Projection` trait, the runner, ADR-0020/0021 and PS-33's falsifier are `typed-layer-and-alpha-release`'s, HS-P0011). Every rule those clauses name is integration-level. So the decidable content here is narrow and worth stating as the decision it is: **the port grows nothing for apply failure.**

- The skip primitive **already exists and nobody has noticed it**: `begin()` followed immediately by `commit(batch, id, poison_position, Live)` applies nothing and advances the checkpoint atomically, with the port exactly as written. What does not exist is any way to *record* that it happened, and routing the record through the projection's own batch means no new port surface and no store-side knowledge of what a skip means (PS-27, `:5411-5427`).
- `rollback` must survive the port change, and PS-30 is why: a fan-out runner wrapping `&mut P::Batch` in `AssertUnwindSafe` is defensible *only* because `rollback` exists — `rollback` is what discharges the promise (`:5462-5478`).
- The failure policy is per projection, not per runner (PS-26, `[FROZEN]`), and isolation already works structurally: `Projection::Store` is an associated type and checkpoints are per `(store, ProjectionId)` (PS-29).
- What is **deferred by name**: `PumpError<E::Error, P::Error, A>`'s three type parameters, the `SkipAndRecord` vocabulary, and the supervisor's observability half (PS-28, PS-29's second half) — HS-P0011's, with ADR-0020/0021 as the vehicle.

An ADR-0019 that designs the runner's error type has taken another project's scope and will be reversed by it.

### 6. The authoring path — intake, then a human-invoked wave

`.kb/decisions/` atoms are authored by `/redkiln:kb-ingest` from `.kb/_intake/`, not by hand (`CLAUDE.md`, *Where the work lives*). A decision lives in two places on purpose: `references/adr/` holds the full original record — compiler transcripts, rejected alternatives, measurement tables, up to 1,508 lines — and `.kb/decisions/` holds the ~100-line atom carrying the frontmatter, status and supersession graph `validate --kb` enforces. **Link the atom; cite the record by `file:line`.** So the story writes the long form first, stages it, and hands off:

1. `references/adr/0017-*.md`, `0018-*.md`, `0019-*.md` — the records, in the corpus's existing house shape (`references/adr/0010-the-suite-must-prove-itself.md` is the nearest model for an ADR whose subject is proof obligations).
2. `.kb/_intake/*.md` — the staged material for the wave. `_intake` is skipped by `validate --kb` precisely so a draft need not satisfy the atom schema to sit there; a file still sitting there after a run is a file that run did not ingest (`.kb/_intake/README.md`).
3. `/redkiln:kb-ingest` — **user-invoked**, run on its own worktree branch, clearing `_intake`, wiring reciprocal backlinks, syncing the maps and running `redkiln validate --kb` + `redkiln doctor`. Its glob picks up `.kb/_intake/README.md`; drop it at the approval gate. Give the wave id a suffix that does not collide with `2026-08-10-intake` / `2026-08-10-intake-2` under `.kb/_governance/integration-waves/`, or the second wave overwrites the first's audit trail.

**The status vocabulary is a live open question — do not settle it in passing.** `KbFrontmatter`'s `status` is a closed enum and the corpus's "accepted — provisional" has no value in it. The convention applied by the 2026-08-10 import, and the one these three atoms follow, is: `status: accepted`, with the qualification **and its falsifier** folded into the first clause of `summary`, and `supersedes`/`superseded_by` reserved for *full* supersession only. Do not invent frontmatter keys, and do not answer `kb-open-question-adr-status-vocabulary-001` by acting on it (`.kb/open-questions/adr-status-vocabulary-exceeds-the-schema.md`).

### 8. The input from the dependency, and the ordering that is the check

**`ps-clause-pairing-sweep` (HS-S0001) lands first and its finding scopes these ADRs.** A systematic pairing defect and two isolated ones want different repairs, and the whole reason the sweep is a separate story is that widening ten frozen clauses under cover of a rule-writing story is a re-plan, not a wider ADR written quietly (`_decomposition.md` Architecture brief Note 8; Note 10 item 4). Each ADR therefore states whether any clause in its own range carries the defect and **defers the repair**; if the sweep returned "systematic", this story reports at its boundary rather than absorbing it.

**And the ordering.** AC-008 says the three atoms are accepted *before the port change lands*. The instrument is a commit sequence plus two commands: `redkiln validate --kb` green on the tree that carries the atoms and **not** the port change, and `git diff --diff-filter=D` over `.kb/open-questions/` empty (`_decomposition.md` Testing brief, AC-008 row). `owned-batch-port-shape` (HS-S0003) is blocked on this story for exactly that reason — the ordering *is* the check (`_storymap.md` Coverage, AC-008 row).

### 9. The persona slice

One persona, indirectly. **P2, the adapter author**, wants "an executable definition of 'correct' they can run against their own storage system, rather than a prose specification they have to interpret", and fears "that the port quietly assumed something their storage cannot provide, discovered late" (`_decomposition.md` UX brief, persona table). What it ships is the record that says *which* assumption was made and what was weighed against it — so that when the port does bite, the answer is a citation rather than an archaeology exercise. ## Implementation notes (non-prescriptive)

Sequencing that reduces rework, none of it binding:

- **Read the sweep's finding first.** It is the input that scopes all three records (`ps-clause-pairing-sweep/spec.md`, and its report once written). Writing ADR-0018 before knowing whether PS-19's defect is systematic risks a scope sentence that has to be re-cut.
- **Write the long form first, at full length.** The atom is a distillation of the record, not a draft of it. Working the other way tends to produce a record that is the atom with padding — and the record is where the transcripts have to live.
- **`references/adr/0010-the-suite-must-prove-itself.md` is the nearest shape model** — an ADR whose subject is proof obligations rather than a type signature, which is what ADR-0019 mostly is. `references/adr/0009-error-send-sync.md` is the model for *how* a transcript is quoted.
- **A useful order inside each record**: the question as the runbook posed it → what the compiler said (quoted) → the decision → the alternatives and why each lost → what is provisional, with its falsifier and phase → what is explicitly deferred and to whom.
- **ADR-0019 is the one most likely to overrun.** Its clause range is mostly about a runner that is not this project's. Draft its *Out of scope* paragraph early and check every later paragraph against it.
- **Stage all three intake documents in one wave.** Three waves would produce three audit trails for one decision set and three chances at an id collision.
- **The implementation report is part of the deliverable**, not a summary of it: it carries the six-question owner table, the sweep's verdict as received, and the commit sha at which `redkiln validate --kb` was green with `projection.rs` unchanged.
- **Do not run `/redkiln:kb-ingest` from inside the implementation.** It is user-invoked (`.claude` memory, *Redkiln SDLC commands are user-invoked*); plan it as a handoff at the wave gate.

## Clarifications resolved during spec

1. **The AC set is exactly the ten the front half enumerated** — AC-001 … AC-010. None added, none dropped. The Behavior-and-interfaces table's twelve rows fold into ten: "each record quotes its evidence" and "one decision per title" became AC-004 and AC-006; "sweep finding consumed" folded into **AC-006**, because both are the same obligation — do not let a conjunction or an upstream verdict silently widen a record's scope; "atom frontmatter conforms" folded into **AC-007**, since the frontmatter convention is a property of what the wave produces; and "nothing executable changes" folded into **AC-010**, where it is the same `git diff` assertion that proves the ordering.
2. **Verification is Static and review-of-artefact throughout, and that is the brief's own answer, not a weakening.** `_decomposition.md`'s Testing brief AC-008 row calls it "a process gate on commit sequence as much as a schema check". There is no `#[test]` that can observe a decision record, and inventing a test file to satisfy a column would be a decorative rule of exactly the kind `CLAUDE.md` forbids. Each ledger row's `verifying_test` therefore names a real command plus the real path it reads.
3. **`cargo xtask ci --fast` is recorded as a non-regression check, not as evidence.** It compiles a tree this story does not touch. Listing it as proof of anything would be a false positive by construction.
4. **The composition family of the interaction-quality invariants is discharged by `_design.md`, not skipped.** `surfaces: []` is a signed-off determination and the sign-off block says the no-surface determination itself is what was approved. The two invariants that survive translation into the corpus medium — presentation exists at all, and the density budget — are bound to AC-005/AC-007 and AC-004/AC-007 respectively, so they are gated rather than asserted in prose.
5. **The `.kb/` atom ids and the file names differ, deliberately.** Context pack §7 cites atoms by `id` (e.g. `kb-open-question-ps-19-scope-narrower-001`) while the anchors table cites paths (`.kb/open-questions/ps-19-scope-narrower-than-its-rule.md`). Both are correct and both were verified to exist; the id is what `related` links resolve against, the path is what an implementer opens.
6. **Three atoms, three records, one wave.** Splitting into three waves was considered and rejected in the notes: it multiplies the audit trails and the id-collision surface for one decision set. Splitting a *record* remains available and is AC-006's explicit remedy if a title's two halves cannot be told apart in strength.
7. **`LiveHandleProjectionStore`'s disposition is required to be *named*, not required to be a particular value.** AC-001 accepts deletion, a move to `experiments/`, or retention with a note — what it rejects is silence. Architecture brief AC-A05 and Note 7 are the source; this spec does not pre-empt the choice.
