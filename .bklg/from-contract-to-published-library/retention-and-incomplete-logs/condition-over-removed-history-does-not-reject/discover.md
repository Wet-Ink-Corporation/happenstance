## Questions

- **Is "fails loudly" expressible in the existing error vocabulary?** **Answered here, and this is the story where the answer is sharpest: no — and ES-40 says so on purpose.** `Guard::is_violated_by` returns `bool` from a two-arm `match` (`crates/happenstance-core/src/append.rs:249-252`); there is no third arm and no place to put one without a new variant on the condition-evaluation result, which is DA-7's third row (`_decomposition.md:373`) and a `0.3.0`. **So this rule does not attempt to make the failure loud. It asserts that it is silent, and documents the silence.** That is not a workaround: ES-40's own text says the store MAY admit the append and the contract MUST NOT imply otherwise, so making it loud would contradict the clause this story discharges. The loudness owed to AC-006 is a *reader's* problem and is HS-S0118's and HS-S0119's; the escalation, if one is warranted, is HS-S0124's.
- **DT-7 — one signal or three?** **Deferred to HS-S0120**, and this story is written to survive either answer. The rule asserts an outcome (`the append is admitted`), not the presence or shape of any incompleteness signal, so no DT-7 answer changes it. What this story *owes* DT-7 is the observation that the vacuous pass is the same value under every kind of hole — transient, benign-permanent or meaningful-permanent — which is evidence that the distinction, if made, cannot be made here.
- **Where does the rustdoc sentence go?** **Deferred to `spec`, with the candidates named:** beside `AppendCondition::is_violated_by` in `crates/happenstance-core/src/append.rs`, and/or on `EventStore::append` in `crates/happenstance-core/src/store.rs:213`. ES-40 asks for two audiences — an adapter author reading the contract and an ingest author reading the hazard — which argues for both sites with one of them linking the other. `spec-trace`'s citation check (`xtask/src/spec_trace.rs:729-733`) resolves the clause's `file:line` citations, so the placement and the clause's citation must be reconciled in the same change.
- **Does the mutant reject, or error?** **Deferred to `spec`, and the answer must be *one* of them.** A `Defect` that rejects models the adapter that fails closed on a detected gap; one that errors models the adapter that treats a gap as an infrastructure fault. Both are plausible, and `mutants_fail_exactly_their_declared_rules` (`crates/happenstance-testkit/tests/mutation_coverage.rs:2889`) plus the one-defect-per-store discipline mean they should not be the same `Defect`. Pick the one that fails *exactly* this rule; if both do, the second is a second row, not a second behaviour on the first.
- **Does this story touch `Guard` or `AppendCondition`'s behaviour?** **Answered: no.** The predicate already behaves as ES-40 specifies. This story asserts and documents existing behaviour; a change to `is_violated_by` would be a change to a published surface and is out of scope by gate decision 4.

## Decision

An `AppendCondition` is a claim about the log the evaluating store holds, not about the world — and where matching history has been removed, the information is missing from the store rather than merely from the API, so `Guard::is_violated_by` returns `false` and the append is admitted. That is not a bug: it is what a pure predicate over events that still exist must do, and `crates/happenstance-core/src/append.rs:239-253` has two arms and nowhere to put a third. Until now nothing could stage the situation, which is why ES-40 carries `[PROVISIONAL]` naming CF-27's instrument as its falsifier. This story writes `condition_over_removed_history_does_not_reject` against that instrument, asserting the vacuous pass as **specified** behaviour, and lands the documentation sentence ES-40 actually requires. The spec will cover: the rule's assertion — a condition whose query ranges over destroyed history admits the append, compared against positions the store assigned rather than literals; its registry line in `for_each_event_store_rule!`; a `Defect` that fails closed instead, with its own `REGISTRY` row and a non-empty `fails` list, and both meta-tests green in both directions; and the rustdoc on `AppendCondition` and/or `EventStore::append` written for two readers at once — an adapter author who must read the vacuous pass as the contract, and an ingest author who must read it as a hazard — with no signature change anywhere in `happenstance-core`, which is what makes ES-40 the clause that proves the no-surface-change constraint is affordable.

## The wrong implementation

**`GapAwareRejectingStore` — an adapter that notices its own gap and fails closed.** It is the sympathetic mutant, and that is exactly why it is the dangerous one: an author who knows the store has been pruned, and who knows a condition may therefore be unsound, makes the condition **reject** rather than admit — or returns an error naming the gap — because failing closed is the safer-sounding choice. It passes every check in the workspace today, because `condition_over_removed_history_does_not_reject` does not exist in `for_each_event_store_rule!` (`crates/happenstance-testkit/src/registry.rs:94`) and no fixture can produce a gap to notice. It is wrong because it contradicts the clause directly: ES-40 says the store **MAY** admit the append and the contract **MUST NOT** imply otherwise (`spec/SPECIFICATION.md:4356-4357`), so a store that rejects has silently converted a `MAY` into a prohibition, and every writer against it now sees a spurious conflict for a decision model the store cannot evaluate. **Its home is `crates/happenstance-testkit/tests/mutation_coverage/mutants.rs` with a `Declared` row in `REGISTRY` at `crates/happenstance-testkit/tests/mutation_coverage.rs:324`.**

Two more that would leave the gate green, and that the spec must be written against:

- **A rule that asserts the *desired* outcome instead of the specified one.** Written by an author who thinks the vacuous pass is a defect, this rule would assert that a condition over removed history *rejects* — and it would be green against the mutant and red against every conformant store, inverting the suite. AC-005's wording exists for this: *"asserts the specified outcome"*. If the rule seems wrong when it is written, the answer is not to soften it; it is ES-39's escalation, recorded, with the rule still asserting what the specification says.
- **A documentation sentence that describes the mechanism but never names the hazard.** ES-40 is discharged by prose, and prose passes every compiler. A sentence saying "conditions are evaluated over the local log" is true, gate-green, and useless to the ingest author who is the second audience the clause names (`spec/SPECIFICATION.md:4368-4371`). The check is whether an ingest author reading it would recognise their own situation — the `Rejects:` case at `:4373-4379`, which under the unconditional-ingest decision is the *normal* path, not an edge one.
