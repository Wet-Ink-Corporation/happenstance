## Questions

- **Is "fails loudly" expressible in the existing error vocabulary?** **Answered upstream and *recorded* here — that recording is this story's substantive work.** The answer is no, and ES-39's marker is where the specification has to say so: its own exit condition asks which of the three candidate primitives an ingest path and a projection runner each need, and the evidence from HS-S0118 and HS-S0119 is that each needs one that does not exist, so the clause leaves `[DEFERRED]` for a stated reason and not by a mechanical status flip. What replaces the marker must not imply a primitive was chosen — no primitive was, and choosing one is a `0.3.0` outside this initiative's exit criteria.
- **What replaces `[DEFERRED]` on ES-39 and CF-27?** **Deferred to `spec`, and it is the story's central content decision.** The marker vocabulary is the specification's own (`frozen`, `provisional`, `deferred`, or demotion to non-normative prose, per `CLAUDE.md`). CF-27's replacement is the easier one: the experiment ran, the instrument exists, the pass list is recorded. ES-39's is harder: the *question* is answered — by ADR-0028, in the negative, inside a stated constraint — while the *primitive* remains unchosen, so the replacement text must distinguish "settled" from "implemented" and cite ADR-0028 for the difference.
- **Discharge or renew ES-40's `[PROVISIONAL]`?** **Deferred to `spec`, with the mechanism fixed:** the falsifier named in the marker is CF-27's suffix store, and it now exists and gave the clause its assertion (HS-S0117). If the marker is renewed rather than discharged, it must name an experiment — an unnamed renewal is a CF-38 gate failure by construction, the same bar AC-010 is held to.
- **Does anything in §1.3 change, and who computes it?** **Answered: yes, and a human.** Moving a marker changes the maturity census. `spec-trace --write` regenerates §7.1/§7.2; §1.3's prose numbers — the total, each maturity count, the "of which N are normative" figure and the document's own subtraction relating the three — are hand-edited in the same commit, deliberately, because generating them would destroy the property they are being used to prove (`xtask/src/spec_trace.rs:39-54`).
- **DT-7 — one signal or three?** **Answered upstream by HS-S0120 and inherited.** It reaches this story only insofar as ES-39's replacement marker must not smuggle in a signal shape as though the specification had adopted one.
- **May this story edit a `[FROZEN]` clause's `Rule:` line?** **Answered: no, and it does not need to.** Escalated at the `/redkiln:plan` discover gate and decided by the repository owner on 2026-08-12: **reading (b)**. `spec-trace` check 4 (`xtask/src/spec_trace.rs:1626-1631`) *permits* a `(new)` marker rather than requiring its removal, so this story is scoped to leave **ES-38's text untouched entirely**. The storymap's *"the `(new)` markers come off the `Rule:` lines whose rules now exist"* (`_storymap.md:77`) is honoured for every non-frozen clause and is treated as a preference, not an obligation, where the clause is frozen. No ADR is owed, because nothing frozen is edited. Readings (a) and (c) are recorded below as the alternatives that lost.

## Decision

Three clause markers in `spec/SPECIFICATION.md` are stalled on an instrument that now exists and an experiment that has now been run, and one of the repository's gate steps exists precisely so those markers cannot rot into decoration. This story moves them and proves the result: ES-39 and CF-27 leave `[DEFERRED]` — CF-27 because its experiment ran and its pass list is recorded, ES-39 because ADR-0028 answered its question in the negative inside a stated constraint, without choosing a primitive — and ES-40's `[PROVISIONAL]` falsifier is either discharged, now that the suffix store gave the clause its assertion, or renewed against a named experiment. The spec will cover: the replacement text for each marker, written so that "settled" is legibly distinct from "implemented" and no primitive is implied to have been chosen; the disposition of the `(new)` annotations on the `Rule:` lines whose rules now exist, including the ES-38 question raised below; the ordering of the commit — `cargo xtask spec-trace --write` to regenerate §7.1/§7.2, then a hand reconciliation of §1.3's prose census, then `cargo xtask spec-trace` to check — all in one commit, because a marker move committed without the tables, or tables regenerated without §1.3, is green in both wrong cases until the next unrelated marker move; and `cargo xtask ci --fast` green on the result.

## The wrong implementation

**`MarkerFlip` — a change that removes `[DEFERRED]` from ES-39 and CF-27, runs `cargo xtask spec-trace --write`, and commits.** It satisfies every existing check: §7.1 and §7.2 are regenerated so their equality check passes, no rule is unclaimed, every citation resolves, the gate is green and `cargo xtask ci --fast` passes. It is wrong in two ways, and the second is worse than the first. **First**, §1.3's prose census now disagrees with the document and nothing says so — the counts are computed by a human by design, and the tool checks them rather than writing them precisely so a parser regression cannot shift the census and the table together and stay green (`xtask/src/spec_trace.rs:39-54`). A `MarkerFlip` commit is green today and leaves the next author's unrelated marker move failing for a reason that is not theirs. **Second**, and this is the sharper mutant: **`SettledByAssertion`** — a replacement marker whose text reads as though ES-39's primitive had been chosen. Nothing checks marker *prose*. `spec-trace` verifies markers exist, that deferrals and provisional clauses name what they are waiting on, that rules resolve and are claimed, and that the tables agree; it cannot tell a clause that records "no primitive was adopted, and here is why" from one that records "the retained-ranges primitive is adopted", and the second would settle ES-39 in the specification while the published port stays exactly as it was. That is a documented promise no code keeps — a breach of gate decision 4 achieved entirely in prose, and the specific failure this story must be written against.

Two smaller ones, both green:

- **`RenewalWithoutAnExperiment`.** ES-40's `[PROVISIONAL]` marker is renewed with a vague "pending further work". CF-38 makes an unnamed falsifier a gate failure by construction — the same bar AC-010 is held to — so this is the branch that must name a real experiment or discharge the marker outright.
- **`RetiredButLive`.** A rule removed from a clause while it still runs in the registry, caught by `cargo xtask lints`' *"no retired rule is still live"* step. This project should retire nothing; the note exists so that "should" is checked rather than assumed.

This story adds no conformance rule and registers no mutant. Its executable checks are `cargo xtask spec-trace` and `cargo xtask ci --fast`; the mutants above are all things those checks pass, which is why the spec must state the commit's ordering and the §1.3 hand-edit as steps rather than as habits.

Three readings are available and the spec stage must pick one **on the record** rather than in passing: (a) the `(new)` annotation is a factual bookkeeping marker rather than clause content, so removing it once the rule exists is a reconciliation and not a change — in which case say who decided that and where; (b) the annotation stays, because `spec-trace` check 4 (`xtask/src/spec_trace.rs:1626-1631`) *permits* a `(new)` marker rather than requiring its removal, so leaving it is gate-safe and the storymap's line is a preference — in which case ES-38's text is not touched at all and this box becomes tickable as written; or (c) it genuinely is a change to a frozen clause, and it needs ADR-0028 (or a successor) to authorise it in writing, first. **Preference, stated for the spec stage:** (b) is the smallest honest move and the one that keeps this box tickable; (a) is defensible but must be recorded; (c) is the escalation. The same question does **not** arise for ES-39, CF-27 or ES-40, none of which is frozen.

**The other judgement call.** *Literal position values*: this story adds no conformance rule and edits no test — it edits specification prose and regenerates two tables. It is ticked as satisfied, and the box's spirit is carried into the marker text: ES-39's replacement must not describe incompleteness as a boundary position, because a floor is `earliest_position()` and the clause's own argument is that a regulated purge is *scattered, not a prefix* (`spec/SPECIFICATION.md:4336-4342`).
