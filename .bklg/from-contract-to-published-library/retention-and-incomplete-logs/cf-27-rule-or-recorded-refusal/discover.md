## Questions

- **Is "fails loudly" expressible in the existing error vocabulary?** **Answered, and this is the story where the answer determines what gets built.** No. `suffix_store_is_distinguishable_from_a_young_store` asks a store to be *distinguishable* — that is, to say something about its own completeness — and there is nowhere in the port for it to say it: `EventStore` has `read`, `append`, `head` and `contains_event_id`, and none carries a completeness channel (`crates/happenstance-core/src/store.rs:119`, `:213`, `:268`). CF-27 itself makes the rule's assertion depend on ES-39's primitive (`spec/SPECIFICATION.md:8042-8044`), and every candidate primitive is a new port surface (DA-7). **So inside gate decision 4 this rule cannot be written honestly**, and the branch this story most likely takes is the refusal branch: the rule is not written, CF-27's `Rule:` line keeps its `(new)` marker, and the clause records why it cannot exist and what a reader is therefore on its own against. That refusal is a legitimate answer the runbook already recognises (`RUNBOOK.md:517`), and it is *not* the cheap branch — DR-9 keeps the instrument and the illustration either way.
- **Which branch does this story take?** **Deferred to ADR-0028 by construction — this story consumes the decision and does not make it.** Its spec must specify **both** branches completely enough that either can be executed the day HS-S0121 lands, because a story that has planned only the branch it expects will improvise the other one at the worst moment.
- **If the decide branch is taken, what happens?** **Answered: it does not proceed silently.** Writing the rule presupposes a primitive; adopting a primitive is a change to a surface published at `0.2.0`. That is an AC-012 escalation naming the surface, the version consequence and DA-7's four rows — raised through HS-S0124 — and **no change is made here**. `project.md:184-185` states it plainly: a conclusion that a surface change is required is a finding, not a licence.
- **Could the rule be written against something weaker than a full primitive?** **Deferred to `spec`, with a warning attached.** A rule that distinguishes the instrument from a young store using something *only the instrument* exposes — a test-side accessor, a fixture capability, a downcast — would satisfy CF-27's name and none of its meaning: it would prove the instrument knows what it hid, which was never in doubt. DA-4's line applies (`_decomposition.md:299-305`): what a store does not hold is a **fact** with no home on `Fixture`, because putting it there answers ES-39 in a place no application can read.
- **DT-7 — one signal or three?** **Answered upstream by HS-S0120 and inherited.** It bears on this story only in the decide branch, where it would fix the *shape* of what the rule asserts. In the refusal branch it becomes part of what is recorded as unavailable.

## Decision

CF-27 names a rule whose assertion, by the clause's own words, is fixed by the retention decision — so until ADR-0028 exists the rule has no content, and once ADR-0028 exists the rule either has one or provably cannot. This story settles it either way. Because a store can only be distinguishable from a young one by *reporting* something, and every reporting primitive ES-39 contemplates is a change to a port published at `0.2.0`, the branch reachable inside gate decision 4 is the refusal: the rule is not written, CF-27's `Rule:` line keeps its `(new)` marker so `spec-trace` check 4 stays green, and the clause text records in writing why the rule cannot exist and what a reader is therefore on its own against. The spec will cover both branches in full: for the decide branch, the rule body in `crates/happenstance-testkit/src/suite.rs`, its single registry line, the assertion as ADR-0028 fixes it, its registered wrong implementation, and the AC-012 escalation that must precede it because a primitive is a published-surface change; for the refusal branch, the exact clause text — what cannot be checked, why, and what a reader is left holding — plus the confirmation that no rule name enters the registry unclaimed (check 6) and that CF-27 continues to satisfy check 4 by keeping its marker.

## The wrong implementation

**`SelfReportingSuffixStore` — an instrument that satisfies `suffix_store_is_distinguishable_from_a_young_store` by telling the *test* what it hid.** A method on the concrete instrument type, or a `Capability` on its `Fixture`, or a downcast in the rule body: any of these makes the rule green, gets it claimed by CF-27 so `spec-trace` check 6 passes, and gets it registered so it runs in every emitter. It satisfies every existing check and is worthless, because the property it proves is that the instrument remembers its own retained predicate — which was never in question — while the property CF-27 asks about is whether an *application* holding only an `impl EventStore` can tell. It also does real damage: it answers ES-39 in a place no application can read (`_decomposition.md:299-305`), and it makes the completeness axis look closed while the far end is still empty. **If it is built at all, it belongs nowhere — but the shape to watch for lives in `crates/happenstance-testkit/tests/` beside the instrument, and the defence written into the spec is that the rule body may use only `Fixture`'s declared surface and the `EventStore` port, exactly as every other rule in `suite.rs` does.**

Two more, one per branch:

- **Refusal branch — `SilentNonRule`.** ADR-0028 refuses, the rule is simply never mentioned again, CF-27 keeps its `(new)` marker and the gate is green: check 4 is satisfied by the marker, check 6 has no unclaimed rule to complain about, `cargo xtask ci --fast` passes. Nothing is wrong except that the reader who comes to CF-27 next year finds a rule that has been "new" for two years and no record of why. AC-007's second half exists for this — the refusal must record *in writing* why the rule cannot exist and what a reader is on its own against.
- **Decide branch — `QuietPrimitive`.** The rule is written, and to make it assertable a small method is added to `EventStore` — one signature, additive, a minor bump under 0.x. Every gate stays green: `cargo-semver-checks` reports additive, `spec-trace` is happy, the suite passes. And a port published at `0.2.0` has changed, which gate decision 4 forbids and AC-012 converts into an escalation. The check `_decomposition.md:614` names is mechanical: no diff touches the public signatures in `crates/happenstance-core/src/store.rs` or `append.rs`.
