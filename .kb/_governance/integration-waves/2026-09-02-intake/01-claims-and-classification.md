# Wave `2026-09-02-intake` — claims and classification

Every claim the seven staged files carry, labelled against the **accepted decision corpus** and
routed to the operation that absorbs it. The labels are the four the ingest defines:

- **aligns** — the claim is consistent with an accepted decision and adds no obligation to it.
- **extends** — the claim adds knowledge no accepted decision holds, without contradicting one.
- **conflicts** — the claim cannot be true at the same time as an accepted decision's body.
- **requires-new-decision** — the claim asks for a commitment nobody has made.

**Zero claims in this wave are labelled `conflicts`**, and this time the hunt for one had a real
candidate. Two staged sentences about `happenstance-neon` and `happenstance-postgres` are
**false against the tree** (`00`, Corrections 1 and 2). That is not a conflict: they disagree
with mutable manifests and with a specification clause, not with an accepted decision's body,
and the corpus's own instrument — `spec/SPECIFICATION.md`:392 — already carries the right
answer. Corrected in place, at the point of placement, and recorded rather than absorbed.

The other thing worth stating before the tables: **four of the seven files carry commitments in
their own voice for the first time in this corpus's history on a non-architecture subject.**
*"Seven blocks, always"*, *"the wordmark stays lowercase"*, *"a standalone SVG must carry one
fixed colour"*, *"never assert that the name is clever"*. KB authority rule 2 makes no exception
for subject matter — a *must* is a decision atom or it is unenforceable — and that is what sends
brand material into `.kb/decisions/` rather than into a concept or a playbook. `02`,
Adjudication 6.

**All five `requires-new-decision` claims arrive taken, and that is why all five become decision
atoms rather than open questions.** This is the discriminator wave 6 drew between its two, and it
is worth restating because this wave is the first to apply it five times running. ADR-0035
arrives with the exemption already written into `deny.toml` and its long-form record on disk;
PS-3's verdict arrives with PS-2's bar evaluated part by part, the rejected arm named and its
asymmetric cost stated; the three brand commitments arrive with their alternatives rejected,
their costs acknowledged, and — in SD-0002's case — the rendering that produced each rejection.
None of them hands the wave a fork with both tines named and neither chosen, which is the
condition under which wave 6 refused to sign the very exemption Op 2 now transcribes.

**The one claim in this wave that does hand over a fork is `ps-3` C3**, and it becomes an open
question for exactly that reason: whether `projection-store` leaves the `default` set of two
unpublished crates is a crate-surface decision with a consumer consequence, nobody has taken it,
and the file names the owner and the forcing event rather than the answer.
